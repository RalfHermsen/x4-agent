# Plan: steady growth by conquest, one faction at a time

Written 2026-11-06 at about 425 game hours, after a review of the whole repo and
all run data by four reviewers (military, economy, engineering, direction). Read
`CLAUDE.md` first, then `tasks/lessons.md`, then this file. Numbers marked
(inferred) were not measured directly.

The aim: grow slowly and steadily at the expense of hostile factions. One faction
at a time; its sectors taken one by one; every sector taken becomes a base with
production, trade, a patrol and, where needed, its own shipbuilding. The next
sector is only attacked once the last one holds.

## What the review found

* Freighters are the largest loss and nothing lowered it. start8 and start9 lost
  86 container freighters, 62 of them to the Scale Plate Pact. Pact kills of our
  freighters and miners rose from about 0.08 per game hour (start8b to start8o,
  120 h) to about 0.23 (start8p to start9m, 229 h), from the run in which the Pact
  were made enemies (-0.032) and their sectors raided. In the same period our
  traders went from 8 to 18, so the cause is not separated. The guard, the route
  frigates and two escorts per trader did not lower the rate: a guard holds a
  sector, not a route.
* Heavy ships live, light ships die. No destroyer was lost since start8m (the 12
  before were all lost to the Xenon). Since start9h, 9 frigates and about 40
  fighters were lost to the Kha'ak. 644 Kha'ak were killed in start9, mostly S and
  M fighters. The empty carrier SAP-470 has the most kills of any ship (97).
* Our mining raises Kha'ak activity (`md/khaak_activity.xml`), and many miners
  have nowhere to deliver: ZLR-942's and GZI-064's solid stores are full and
  miners report "no buyers". 15 miners were lost to the Kha'ak.
* The economy is about flat over the game. Net worth at cost: 1.0 bn at the start,
  627 M at its lowest (125 h), about 1.15 bn at 422 h. Credits rose 0.7 M per game
  hour from 148 to 338 h and 2.8 M per hour from 345 to 425 h, gross of what was
  built. 0.9 bn credits sit idle. Supply is ahead of the demand we reach: ZLR-942
  runs 28 traders for a plan of 18, and "no trades found" is frequent.
* The largest visible price gap: HWE-974 bids 211 to 221 Cr for about 28,000
  refined metals while ZXX-974 and GZI-064 offer theirs at about 94.
* One construction vessel holds every build in line: GZI-064 has waited for its
  S/M bay since it was claimed at 338 h.
* Today's bugs are one class. A run starts with empty memory, and several rules
  pick the same ships: the pier ordered four times, the guard sent home at a
  restart, a second station calling the guard to itself, `fleet` counting raid
  ships at every station. Every fix so far was one rule at a time. No test runs
  all rules together on a realistic state, and nothing checks a restart before it
  happens.
* The README describes an agent "driven by a local LLM". What runs is rules plus a
  plan file written by hand. The old yardstick (the game alone, 1.82 M per game
  hour) does not carry over to this game, so there is no baseline.

## Principles

1. **One campaign at a time.** One target faction, one target sector. A second
   faction is not provoked while a campaign runs.
2. **Hold before taking.** A sector is attacked only when the last one taken has
   passed its gate (Phase 2, step f).
3. **Every sector taken is a base:** claimed, defended, producing, trading,
   patrolled, and building ships where that is shorter than bringing them.
4. **Heavy ships fight, light ships guard stations.** Destroyers do the presence
   in hostile space; fighters defend stations; frigates do not lead fighters into
   Kha'ak space.
5. **Measured, not felt.** Every change is judged against the yardstick of Phase
   1.7: net worth at cost per game hour, losses per game hour by type and
   attacker, credits per game hour.

## Phase 0: the foundation (no new behaviour)

Order matters; each step makes the next safer. None of these change what the agent
does in the game.

### 0.1 A dry run before every restart
`experiment.py --dry-run <save>`: parse the save, run every rule plus
`fleet`/`executor`, print the commands, send nothing. Invariants checked over the
whole command list, as tests and as a warning in live runs:
* no ship in commands from two rules in one cycle;
* no `assign ... defend` or `release` of more than 5 destroyers in one cycle;
* no build ordered twice for one station.

Done when: start9k's save gives the warning "30 destroyers assigned home" in a
test, and every restart in a session has a dry-run log next to it.

### 0.2 Memory that survives a restart
Split `memory.json` into what can be read from the save (leaders, posts, routes,
pending builds) and what cannot (refusals, ledger, offers, sold scouts, swept
sectors, sent marks). Carry the second part from the previous run's folder at
start. Read the first part once per cycle into one "who does what" view that every
rule uses, so the four "taken over from the run before" special cases go.

Done when: a restart in the middle of a raid, a route and a build gives no command
the dry run of the save before it did not give.

### 0.3 One owner per ship per cycle
A `claims` object per cycle: a rule takes a ship before it orders it, a second
take of the same ship fails in tests and logs a warning live. First for scouts
(sweeps, watch, scouts, sell_scouts, revisit), fighters (fighter_posts, patrols,
wings, carriers, fleet) and destroyers (raids, hunts, route_patrol,
undock_fleet, fleet).

### 0.4 Rules without restarts
Reload `standing.py` and `strategy.py` between cycles when they changed; a reload
that fails keeps the old module. A restart is for bridge (Lua/MD) changes only.

### 0.5 Hand commands and diagnoses on record
Hand commands go through a small tool that writes them into the run folder as well
as `logs/outbox.txt`. A short incident checklist at the top of `tasks/lessons.md`
before a cause is claimed: what the order looked like in the save, which sector the
ship was in, whether the thing is waiting in a queue.

### 0.6 A realistic test state
A trimmed, checked-in parsed state from a real save: three stations, a guard of 30
on its post with a leader without its Patrol, a route patrol out, sector patrols,
a pending build with a pier. Tests run all of `RULES` on it and check the
invariants of 0.1.

### 0.7 The sweep keeps flying
The bridge sends the next undiscovered spot when a sweep's MoveWait finishes
(`event_object_order_finished`, as `md/story_unbihexium.xml` uses it) instead of
once per cycle. Done when a scout sweeps Silent Witness XII in under 10 game hours.

## Phase 1: stop the losses, set the yardstick

### 1.1 Miners to what the stores take
ZLR-942 4 solid and 4 liquid to 2 and 2; ZXX-974 and GZI-064 4 solid to 2;
GZI-064 energy cells 4 to 1. Done when no miner reports "no buyers" for 10 game
hours and the Kha'ak kill no miner in that time.

### 1.2 Destroyers for presence, fighters for stations
Sector patrols in Second Contact II Flashpoint and Silent Witness XI become small
destroyer groups (2 to 4) instead of fighter groups led by frigates. Fighters
defend stations (`defence_fighters`). Escorts go from 2 to 1 per trader for one
measured period; the freed fighters guard stations.

### 1.3 Hunts from ships that are not in a group
`hunts` sends destroyers of a station's reserve above its floor, not ships of the
guard: an Attack of a ship under a leader drops when the target is in another
sector (start9j, KET-043), while the reserve's held (start9j, YPX-963, KET-043).

### 1.4 A second construction vessel
About 9 M against 0.9 bn idle. GZI-064's bay and every base of Phase 2 need one
nearby.

### 1.5 Sell where it pays
A trade for refined metals from ZXX-974 and GZI-064 to the best reachable bid
(HWE-974, 211 to 221 Cr) if our traders reach it outside the avoided sectors.
Measure first which of the large gaps (refined metals, field coils, silicon
wafers, plasma conductors) our own traders can serve.

### 1.6 A ceiling on destroyers
No more than today's number (40 at ZXX-974, 14 at ZLR-942). New ones replace
losses or fill the garrisons of Phase 2, nothing else. `fleet` subtracts ships on
order before it builds (it does not today: three bays build side by side past the
number).

### 1.7 The yardstick
One run of 20 game hours with the plan frozen and no hand commands. Measured: net
worth at cost per game hour, credits per game hour, losses per game hour by ship
type and attacker, production lines running against full. These numbers are the
gates of Phase 2.

### 1.8 Say what it is
README and `docs/brief.md` describe what runs: rules and a plan file, the model
optional. Public, so with the player's yes.

## Phase 2: the campaign

### 2.1 In the plan file
A `campaign` section: target faction, target sector, phase, since when. A `bases`
section: one entry per sector taken, from a template (2.3). The rationale keeps
narrating why.

### 2.2 The phases of one sector
a. **Scout.** A scout sweeps the target sector until every station of the
   target faction in it is known; satellites at its gates. Done when the sweep
   reports the sector swept and the known stations of the target do not change
   for 2 game hours.
b. **Siege.** The strike group (today's guard of 30) posts in the sector
   (`raid_guard`), defence platforms first, then stations and build storages,
   hunts against what comes in (1.3). Recall rules as today (hurt, strayed, XL at
   home). Done when the target faction has no station and no build storage left in
   the sector.
c. **Claim.** A plot and a station with the module that claims a sector. In
   Silent Witness XI and XII an L bay first did it; confirm the rule in the game
   files before relying on it (manual before probe). Done when the sector is ours.
d. **Base.** The template of 2.3, built by the nearest construction vessel.
e. **Patrol.** The sector's garrison (2.4) takes over from the strike group:
   destroyers on Patrol, satellites at the gates and stations, the station's own
   fighters. The strike group goes home to rest and repair.
f. **Gate.** The base passes when, over 10 game hours: its production runs at the
   level the yardstick run showed for our other stations, its account does not
   fall, no ship of ours is lost in the sector, the home reserves are whole, and
   net worth at cost is not below where the campaign began. Then the next target
   sector is chosen (2.5). If the gate fails for 30 game hours, the campaign
   pauses and the base is fixed first.

### 2.3 The base template
Defence discs 4; a dock and a pier; energy for its own needs; refining of the
sector's own resources; one product our wharfs use; storage for what it makes; 3
traders; 2 miners per resource it uses; sell limits as at our other stations. An
S/M bay where the front is more than 3 jumps from our nearest bay; an L bay only
at the base nearest the next target.

### 2.4 Strike group, garrisons and reserves
* The strike group: one group of 30 destroyers, moving from sector to sector.
* A garrison per sector held: 2 to 4 destroyers on Patrol, plus each station's
  `defence_fighters`.
* A reserve at home: ZXX-974 10, ZLR-942 6.
* Fleet targets follow from the number of sectors held instead of hand counts:
  strike group + garrisons + reserves.

### 2.5 Choosing the next sector
Within the target faction: the sector nearest to ours with known stations,
fewest defence platforms and XL, and resources our chains use. A second faction is
not touched while one campaign runs. The Kha'ak are not a campaign: they hold no
sectors. Their nests in a sector we hold are cleared when found.

### 2.6 Later: the model proposes
With the yardstick in place, the strategist may propose the next target and a
base template in shadow mode: it writes a plan, the hand plan stays in force, and
the two are compared against the gates.

* **Riptide Rakers.** Own Avarice IV (4 jumps), Avarice I (5) and Avarice V Dead End

jumps), Heretic's End first; then the Vigor; then the Riptide Rakers; the Xenon
last. While the Pact are the target they stay hostile.
